# Proposal defence slides

The authenticated `/defence-slides` workspace prepares ten proposal slides from a saved proposal, a PDF/DOCX/TXT upload, or a topic. Navigation, proposal overview/editor, the student roadmap and full-proposal marking results link to it. Report projects are excluded from saved proposal selection.

Students review extracted facts before generation. The default **Draft missing sections with Gemini** option uses the student's saved research key to draft empty study sections, including topic-only presentations. Turning it off retains the original offline, extractive workflow. Existing non-empty facts, objectives and manual choices are never replaced. Names, HTINs and other unknown identity details are not generated. Longer source details and reference entries belong in speaker notes and the downloadable text handout.

Drafting performs at most one source lookup and two text-generation passes (study focus, then methods), with at most one content/format correction per pass. Search receives only the public topic; candidate identity is excluded from drafting prompts too. Existing proposal text is provided to the text-only requests as study context. References require provider retrieval annotations, not merely a model-written URL. An unavailable/quota-limited search permits a provisional text draft with an explicit evidence warning; authentication/permission failures stop generation. No source, approval, local result or missing identity is manufactured to make the deck appear complete.

Presentation budgets are enforced in `DEFENCE_DRAFT_BUDGETS`: background up to 70 words in three components; problem up to 85 in four; scope up to 65 in content/place/period; literature up to 40; justification/contribution up to 30 each; most methods up to 30 each, with 35 for sample/ethics. Complete points are limited to 240 characters. Objectives retain the exact topic rather than being silently cut to a generic word target. New objectives use shared formulation validation; questions are derived one-for-one, not generated independently. Sample/census consistency and the common 95%/50%/5% single-proportion calculation are checked, but this is not a general statistical or clinical validation engine. Generated facility descriptions cannot invent services or ownership. Unsupported current-situation claims and claims to have reviewed nonexistent evidence are rejected. Actual slide geometry is checked before offering a draft.

Progress reports actual stages, with cancellation and account/navigation cleanup. Failure never replaces the existing presentation. A review dialog displays new sections and word counts before applying them; discarded drafts are not saved. Applying creates a separate presentation version. Suggested facts remain marked `draft: true` in storage, preview/PowerPoint labels and source notes until the student explicitly reviews/confirms each. Editing a suggestion does not automatically confirm it. Proposed methodology and an unconfirmed collection-period assumption are review items, not claims of academic approval or guaranteed marks.

The presentation adapter now reads the same section contracts, citation policies and topic formulation rules as the proposal editor. The problem slide separates ideal situation, current situation/magnitude, gap and consequences/study need. Background separates wider context, local context and gap; scope separates content, place and period; justification and significance retain their different purposes. Narrow, complete ideal/consequence clauses may leave uncited supporting detail in notes. Other overlong or unavailable components stay explicit review/add markers. This is bounded text/formulation checking, not verification of the cited evidence or full academic quality.

Rule review reports objective/question count, topic details, category/construct alignment and citation-policy issues. A separate general question is not counted as a numbered specific question. Shortening approval cannot waive missing population, age, setting, health issue or objective focus. Contradictory displayed aims, objectives/questions or missing problem components block PowerPoint export; source issues and unresolved markers keep the deck labelled as a working draft. Findings remain in the notes/handout. Older saved slides offer an explicit wording comparison and a one-slide update with undo; other edits and original facts are retained.

Each slide has editable title, key points and notes, with its source text available alongside the editor. Restoring one slide uses the reviewed facts without altering other slides. Rehearsal includes navigation, notes and a presentation timer. A changed source proposal is flagged, and creating a new presentation version retains the previous saved deck.

Presentations are saved in IndexedDB under the signed-in user's ID on this device. They are not synced to the private cloud library. Uploads are extracted locally; originals are not retained by this tool. Saving failures are visible, and PowerPoint/notes downloads remain available. Cross-device storage and institutional alternative templates remain future work.

The PowerPoint export uses editable native text, all in black, a white 16:9 canvas, a restrained accent and numbered footers. Each slide has the existing site logo at the top right, with reserved title space and preserved proportions. The embedded PNG is generated from `public/uhpab-logo.svg` by `scripts/sync-defence-logo.mjs`; export tests verify its source hash and image bytes. No network request or browser canvas is needed to export the logo.

Body text automatically selects the largest fitting size from 24, 22, 20 and 18pt; students may instead select a fixed size per slide. Titles can reduce within 32-28pt on the cover and 30-26pt elsewhere. Preview, validation and PowerPoint use the same measurements. No words are deleted and no body text goes below 18pt. Text still too dense at that minimum blocks export. The fixed ten-slide template is an application adaptation, not an official mandated slide order. Incomplete decks carry a working-draft label. A separate Test presentation option marks every slide and the handout as a test version, including otherwise-complete demonstrations.
